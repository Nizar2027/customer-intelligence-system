
# Feature Engineering

## Overview

This stage transforms raw transaction-level retail data into customer-level behavioral datasets that can be used for machine learning tasks. The feature engineering process was designed for a time-based customer intelligence workflow, where historical transactions are used to create input features and future transactions are used to generate target labels.

The outputs of this stage are:
- `churn_dataset.csv`
- `revenue_dataset.csv`

---

## Input Data

The feature engineering process uses two pre-split datasets:

- `train_transactions.csv`: historical transaction data before the cutoff date
- `future_transactions.csv`: future transaction data within the 90-day prediction window

This time-based split ensures that all model inputs come from the past and all targets come from the future.

---

## Objectives

The goal of this stage is to:

1. Build customer-level behavioral features from the training window
2. Build future outcome labels from the future window
3. Merge features and labels into supervised learning datasets
4. Prevent data leakage by excluding future-only variables from model inputs

---

## Preprocessing Steps

### 1. Load Transaction Data

Both training and future transaction files are loaded using Pandas, with `InvoiceDate` parsed as a datetime field.

### 2. Remove Missing Customer Identifiers

Rows with missing `CustomerID` are removed because customer-level features cannot be computed without a valid customer identifier.

### 3. Create Revenue Column

A new `Revenue` field is created as:

`Revenue = Quantity × UnitPrice`

This is used for both historical monetary features and future revenue labels.

---

## Snapshot Date

A snapshot date is defined as one day after the latest transaction date in the training window.

This date is used to calculate recency:

`recency_days = snapshot_date - customer_last_purchase_date`

Using a snapshot date after the last historical transaction ensures a clean temporal reference point.

---

## Customer-Level Features

Historical transactions are aggregated by `CustomerID` to create behavioral features:

### `recency_days`
Number of days since the customer’s most recent purchase in the training window.

### `frequency_orders`
Number of unique orders placed by the customer in the training window.

### `monetary_total`
Total revenue generated by the customer in the training window.

### `total_items`
Total quantity of purchased items in the training window.

### `unique_products`
Number of unique products purchased by the customer.

### `avg_order_value`
Average revenue per order, computed as:

`avg_order_value = monetary_total / frequency_orders`

These features summarize customer behavior and are used as model inputs.

---

## Future Labels

The future window is used only to create targets, not features.

Transactions in the future window are aggregated by `CustomerID` to generate:

### `future_revenue`
Total revenue generated by the customer during the 90-day future window.

### `future_orders`
Number of unique orders placed by the customer during the future window.

---

## Churn Label Definition

A customer is considered churned if they make no purchases during the 90-day future window.

This is encoded as:

- `churn_label = 1` if `future_orders == 0`
- `churn_label = 0` otherwise

Customers with no future activity do not appear in the aggregated future labels table, so after merging, missing future values are filled as:

- `future_revenue = 0`
- `future_orders = 0`

This allows churn labels to be generated consistently for all customers.

---

## Model Dataset Construction

Historical customer features are merged with future labels using a left join on `CustomerID`.

This produces a full supervised learning dataset containing:

- historical input features
- future targets

The resulting intermediate dataset includes:

- `recency_days`
- `frequency_orders`
- `monetary_total`
- `total_items`
- `unique_products`
- `avg_order_value`
- `future_revenue`
- `future_orders`
- `churn_label`

---

## Leakage Prevention

Special care was taken to avoid data leakage.

### Historical Features
All model input features are computed only from the training window.

### Future Labels
All targets are computed only from the future window.

### Removed Future Variables
Before training, future-only helper variables are removed from model-specific datasets:

- `future_revenue` is removed from the churn dataset
- `future_orders` is removed from both the churn and revenue datasets
- `churn_label` is removed from the revenue dataset

This ensures that no future information is used as a predictor.

---

## Final Output Datasets

### Churn Dataset
Contains historical customer features and the binary churn label.

Target:
- `churn_label`

### Revenue Dataset
Contains historical customer features and the continuous future revenue target.

Target:
- `future_revenue`

Both datasets are saved to the `data/processed/` directory.

---

## Why Feature Scaling Was Not Applied

Feature scaling was not applied in this project because the initial models selected for training are:

- `RandomForestClassifier`
- `RandomForestRegressor`

These are tree-based models and are generally not sensitive to differences in feature scale.

## Feature Correlation Analysis

After constructing the customer-level behavioral features, a correlation analysis was performed to better understand the relationships between engineered features and the target variables. The correlation matrix was computed using the final `model_dataset` that contains both historical features and future labels.

The analysis revealed several meaningful patterns that align with expected retail customer behavior.

The `recency_days` feature shows a positive correlation with `churn_label`, indicating that customers who have not purchased recently are more likely to churn. Conversely, `frequency_orders` and `unique_products` show negative correlations with `churn_label`, suggesting that customers who purchase more frequently or interact with a wider variety of products tend to remain active.

For the revenue prediction task, `monetary_total` exhibits the strongest correlation with `future_revenue`, followed by `total_items` and `frequency_orders`. This indicates that customers who historically generate higher revenue or purchase larger quantities are more likely to generate higher revenue in the future.

Some strong correlations were also observed between certain historical features, such as `monetary_total` and `total_items`. This is expected in retail datasets because higher spending customers often purchase larger quantities of items. Since the models used in this project are tree-based algorithms (Random Forest), such correlations do not significantly impact model performance.

Overall, the correlation analysis confirms that the engineered features capture meaningful customer purchasing behavior and provide informative signals for both churn prediction and revenue forecasting tasks.
